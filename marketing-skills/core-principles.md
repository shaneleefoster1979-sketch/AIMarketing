# Core Principles

The non-negotiables that sit underneath every piece of marketing output, regardless of
channel. If a draft violates one of these, fix the draft before anything else.

## 1. Specificity beats cleverness

"Lose 12 lbs in 6 weeks without giving up carbs" outperforms "Transform your body." A vague
promise is a promise nobody believes. Every headline, hook, and bullet should name a person,
a number, a timeframe, or a mechanism.

## 2. One reader, not a demographic

Write to a single person mid-scroll, not to "our audience." If you can't picture the specific
person reading this line — what they searched last night, what they're afraid a friend will
say about them — the copy is still generic. Go back to the avatar before touching the words.

## 3. The problem is emotional; the product is logical

People buy to escape a feeling (embarrassment, anxiety, being stuck) or move toward one
(relief, status, freedom). The product is the logical justification they give themselves and
others afterward. Lead with the feeling, close with the mechanism.

## 4. Every asset has one job

A headline's job is to earn the next sentence. An email subject line's job is to earn the
open. A lead magnet's job is to earn the opt-in. Nothing else. Don't ask a single asset to
educate, convert, and delight simultaneously — that's how you get copy that does nothing
well.

## 5. Clarity beats persuasion

If the reader has to work to understand what's being offered, no amount of persuasive
technique fixes it. Simplify the offer before you polish the pitch.

## 6. Proof outranks assertion

"The best course on the market" is an assertion; a specific result, a number, a named
customer, a screenshot, or a demonstration is proof. Default to showing over claiming in every
piece of copy that makes a promise.

## 7. Test the assumption, not just the headline

A/B testing button colors is optimization theater if the underlying offer or audience
assumption is wrong. Before micro-optimizing an asset, confirm the bigger bet (offer, price,
audience, channel) is validated.

## 8. Distribution is not an afterthought

Great copy with no audience is invisible. Every piece of content or copy should be created
with its distribution channel already decided — don't write first and figure out where it
goes later.

## 9. Protect the relationship, not just the transaction

A list burned by over-selling is worth less than a smaller list that trusts what shows up in
its inbox. Optimize for lifetime trust, not this week's revenue, unless the business
explicitly needs the short-term cash.

## 10. Write it, then cut it in half

First drafts are always longer than they need to be. The edit pass that removes the most
words usually improves the copy the most.
