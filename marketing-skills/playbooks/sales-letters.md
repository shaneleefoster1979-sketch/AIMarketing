# Playbook: Sales Letters & VSL Scripts

Applies to long-form sales pages, video sales letter (VSL) scripts, and webinar pitches.
Assumes `core-principles.md`, `about.md`, and `playbooks/copywriting.md` are already loaded.

## When long-form is the right call

Long-form works when the price or commitment is high enough that the reader needs their
objections handled before they'll act. If the offer is low-price/low-commitment, a short-form
asset (see `copywriting.md`) usually converts better — don't default to long-form out of
habit.

## Structure

1. **Headline + subhead** — the core promise and who it's for, per `copywriting.md`.
2. **Open with the reader's world, not the product.** Describe their current situation
   precisely enough that they think "this was written for me."
3. **Establish the real problem**, including the deeper reason past solutions haven't worked
   (this is often not what the reader initially assumes — e.g. "it's not a discipline problem,
   it's a systems problem").
4. **Introduce the mechanism** — the specific reason this solution works when others haven't.
   Give it a name if possible; a named mechanism is easier to believe and repeat.
5. **Show, don't just tell, the transformation** — case studies, before/afters, screenshots,
   specific numbers from real customers.
6. **Present the offer** — what exactly they get, itemized. Stack the components so the value
   is visible piece by piece before the price appears.
7. **Price framing** — anchor against a relevant comparison (cost of the problem persisting,
   cost of alternatives, cost per day/use) before revealing price.
8. **Handle the top 3–5 objections explicitly.** Name the objection in the reader's words,
   then answer it directly — don't bury the answer inside unrelated copy.
9. **Guarantee** — state it plainly and remove as much risk as the business can genuinely
   afford to remove.
10. **Urgency/scarcity** — real deadline or real limited availability only.
11. **CTA**, repeated at every natural stopping point (end of major sections), not just once
    at the bottom.
12. **P.S.** — restate the core promise and urgency; many readers skip to the P.S. first.

## VSL-specific notes

- Open with the hook in the first 10 seconds — assume most viewers decide whether to keep
  watching before the 30-second mark.
- Write for the ear, not the eye: shorter sentences, more repetition of the core promise than
  a written page would need.
- Script pattern interrupts (tone shift, visual change, direct question to camera) every
  60–90 seconds to reset attention.

## Editing pass checklist

- Does every section earn the next one, or is there a point where a skeptical reader would
  stop?
- Is there a claim without proof directly beside it?
- Could any paragraph be cut without losing an argument the close depends on?
