# Playbook: Email Marketing

Applies to newsletters, nurture sequences, launch sequences, and lifecycle/automation emails.
Assumes `core-principles.md` and `the-fundamentals.md` are already loaded.

## Sequence types and their jobs

| Sequence          | Trigger                          | Job                                              |
|-------------------|-----------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------|
| Welcome            | New opt-in                        | Deliver the lead magnet, set expectations, first win |
| Nurture             | Ongoing, post-welcome              | Build trust and desire before any pitch            |
| Launch              | Scheduled campaign                 | Move a warm list to a specific offer with a deadline |
| Abandonment/cart    | Checkout started, not completed    | Remove friction, answer the likely objection       |
| Post-purchase       | Just bought                        | Deliver a fast first win, reduce refund risk        |
| Win-back            | Inactive for N days                | Re-engage or clean the list                        |

## Welcome sequence (typical shape, 4–7 emails)

1. Deliver the magnet immediately — no delay, no extra hoops.
2. Set expectations: what they'll get from this list, how often, and one quick win now.
3. Tell the founder/brand story — the "why," briefly, focused on credibility and relatability.
4. Address the #1 misconception in the topic area.
5. Social proof — a specific customer result.
6. Soft introduction to the paid offer, framed as "if you want to go further."
7. Direct offer email with a clear CTA, if the sequence includes a pitch.

## Subject lines

- The subject line's only job is the open — don't try to sell in it.
- Specificity and curiosity outperform generic value statements ("5 mistakes killing your
  open rate" beats "Email tips").
- Personalization (name, recent action) helps only when it's genuinely relevant, not just for
  novelty.
- Test emoji/no-emoji and question/statement variants per list — audiences differ.

## Body copy rules

- One primary CTA per email. Secondary links (blog post, social) are fine but should not
  compete visually with the primary CTA.
- Write like one person to one person — email is the most intimate channel available; use
  that instead of writing like a press release.
- Keep paragraphs short (1–3 sentences) — email is read on mobile, scanning, more than any
  other channel.

## Cadence and list health

- Nurture/value emails should outnumber pitch emails outside of active launches — a rough
  default is 3:1 unless the business model requires more frequent selling.
- Segment by engagement; sending real re-engagement or sunset campaigns to cold subscribers
  protects deliverability for the whole list.
- Track open rate, click rate, and (most importantly) revenue/reply per send — vanity opens
  without downstream action mean the list isn't actually engaged.
