# Playbook: Copywriting

Applies to headlines, ad copy, landing page copy, hooks, and any short-form persuasive
writing. Assumes `core-principles.md` and `about.md` are already loaded.

## The core structure: Hook → Problem → Agitate → Solution → Proof → CTA

1. **Hook** — a specific, scroll-stopping first line. Use a number, a contrarian claim, a
   direct callout of the reader, or a surprising result.
2. **Problem** — name the exact situation the reader is in, in their own words, not the
   marketer's words.
3. **Agitate** — show the cost of staying stuck (time, money, status, opportunity), briefly.
   Don't wallow — one or two lines is enough for anything short-form.
4. **Solution** — introduce the mechanism that resolves the problem, framed around the
   outcome, not the feature list.
5. **Proof** — a number, testimonial, credential, or demonstration that makes the claim
   believable.
6. **CTA** — one action, stated plainly. Never present two competing calls to action in the
   same asset.

## Headline formulas that consistently work

- **The direct benefit**: "[Specific outcome] in [specific timeframe], even if [common
  objection]." e.g. "Full pipeline of qualified leads in 30 days, even if you hate cold
  outreach."
- **The contrarian truth**: "Why [common belief] is costing you [specific cost]."
- **The curiosity + specificity combo**: "The [number]-word email that [specific result]."
- **The direct callout**: "If you're a [specific role/situation], read this before
  [specific action]."

Avoid headline formulas that promise nothing specific ("The Ultimate Guide to X",
"Everything You Need to Know About X") — they read as generic even when the content isn't.

## Line-level rules

- One idea per sentence. If a sentence has an "and" joining two claims, consider splitting it.
- Cut qualifiers that weaken a claim without adding accuracy ("kind of," "sort of," "we
  believe," "may help").
- Read every line as the *only* thing the reader will see. If it doesn't stand on its own,
  rewrite it.
- Favor concrete nouns and active verbs. Replace abstractions ("solutions," "results") with
  the actual thing being delivered.

## Common failure modes to check for before shipping

- Feature-dumping instead of leading with outcome.
- A hook that doesn't connect to the actual offer (bait-and-switch, even unintentionally).
- Proof that's vague ("thousands of happy customers") when specific proof is available and
  unused.
- A CTA buried after too much copy, or missing entirely.
