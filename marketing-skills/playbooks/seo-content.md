# Playbook: SEO & Organic Search Content

Applies to review sites, comparison/"best of" content, and any content meant to rank in
search engines rather than (or in addition to) social distribution. This is the primary
channel for a large share of affiliate marketing — search traffic arrives already looking for
a solution, which converts very differently than cold social traffic. Assumes
`core-principles.md` and `the-fundamentals.md` are already loaded; see
`playbooks/affiliate-marketing.md` and `playbooks/content-strategy.md` for adjacent context.

## Search intent comes first, format second

Before writing anything, classify the intent behind the keyword/topic — content that answers
the wrong intent won't rank or convert even if it's well-written:

- **Informational** ("how does X work," "what is Y") — reader is early, not ready to buy.
  Right place for education and for capturing the reader into a lead magnet, not a hard pitch.
- **Commercial investigation** ("best X for Y," "X vs Y," "X review") — reader is comparing
  options and close to a decision. This is the highest-value intent for affiliate content —
  write directly to the comparison/decision, not a general overview.
- **Transactional** ("X discount code," "X pricing," "buy X") — reader has already decided on
  the product and is looking for the best way to get it. Shortest path from click to
  affiliate link; content here should be minimal friction, not persuasive.

Matching format to intent matters more than polish: a "best X for Y" query wants a structured
comparison (see below), not a narrative blog post, and vice versa for informational queries.

## Structuring commercial/comparison content

1. **Answer the decision in the first screen.** State your top pick (or the pick for the most
   common reader segment) near the top, with the reasoning, before the reader has to scroll —
   search readers frequently skim to the verdict first and read supporting detail after.
2. **Segment picks by situation**, not just rank 1-2-3. "Best for beginners," "best value,"
   "best for [specific use case]" — this mirrors how the reader is actually deciding, and lets
   you fairly place multiple different affiliate offers without arbitrarily ranking one above
   another.
3. **Use real, specific criteria**, stated up front — price, key features that actually
   differ between options, who each one is a poor fit for. Per `core-principles.md` #6, a
   comparison with only vague praise across every option isn't useful and won't be trusted.
4. **Personal experience or original testing beats aggregated claims.** If you've used the
   product, say what happened specifically. Search engines and readers both increasingly
   discount reviews that read as templated affiliate copy with no first-hand signal.
5. **Disclose the affiliate relationship**, same requirement as in
   `playbooks/affiliate-marketing.md` — do this before the comparison, not just in a footer.

## On-page basics that matter most

- **Title and H1 should closely match the actual search phrase** the content targets — don't
  bury the topic under a clever headline the way you might for social content (see
  `playbooks/copywriting.md` for where that instinct applies instead).
- **Answer the core question in the first paragraph**, then go deeper — search engines and
  skimming readers both reward getting to the point immediately, unlike a sales letter's
  slower open.
- **Internal links between related content** (a review linking to a broader "best of"
  roundup, and vice versa) help both readers and search engines understand the site's topic
  coverage — don't leave content as orphaned, unconnected pages.
- **Keep content current.** Pricing, features, and even whether a product still exists change
  — outdated comparison content actively damages trust and rankings both. Revisit and update
  older content on a schedule rather than only ever publishing new pages.

## Keyword and topic selection

- Prioritize keywords with clear commercial intent over pure traffic volume — a smaller
  audience that's ready to decide converts far better than a large audience that's just
  browsing.
- Look for topics with a real gap: existing top-ranking content that's outdated, thin, or
  missing a segment of readers (e.g. nothing written for a specific use case or budget tier)
  is easier to outrank than trying to out-write a comprehensive, current top result head-on.
- Cluster related topics (a main comparison page plus individual in-depth reviews of each
  option) rather than one page trying to cover everything — this also creates the internal
  linking structure mentioned above.

## What this channel doesn't replace

SEO content builds compounding traffic over months, not days — it's a poor fit if the need is
immediate revenue this week (use `playbooks/paid-ads.md` or an existing list for that). Treat
organic search as a long-term channel to build alongside, not instead of, paid and
list-based channels — see "Platform and income risk" in `playbooks/affiliate-marketing.md` for
why relying on a single channel is itself a risk.
