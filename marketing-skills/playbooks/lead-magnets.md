# Playbook: Lead Magnets

Applies to opt-ins, freebies, quizzes, challenges, and any asset whose job is converting an
anonymous visitor into a known contact. Assumes `the-fundamentals.md` is already loaded.

## What makes a lead magnet work

- **Solves one problem completely.** Not a preview of a bigger solution — a complete answer to
  a narrow question. "The exact email template that got us a 40% reply rate" beats "A guide to
  email marketing."
- **Fast to consume.** A checklist, template, script, or short video beats a course or ebook
  at this stage — the goal is a quick win, not a study commitment.
- **Directly connected to the paid offer.** The magnet should make the paid offer the obvious
  next step, not a random adjacent topic. If the magnet doesn't naturally lead there, it will
  attract the wrong audience.
- **Specific enough to qualify, not just attract.** A magnet titled for "beginners struggling
  with X" will filter out browsers who aren't the buyer, even at the cost of some raw opt-in
  volume — that trade is usually worth it.

## Common formats, by strength of qualification

1. **Templates/scripts/swipe files** — highest completion rate, very specific promise.
2. **Checklists/cheat sheets** — fast, high perceived value relative to effort to produce.
3. **Short assessment/quiz** — strong engagement and self-segmentation (also collects
   first-party data useful for the nurture sequence).
4. **Mini-course/challenge (3–5 days)** — higher commitment, builds more trust, but lower
   opt-in conversion than a single-asset magnet; best used mid-funnel with a warmer audience.
5. **Webinar/masterclass** — effectively a nurture + offer stage compressed together; treat as
   its own funnel, not just a magnet.

## Landing page for the magnet

- Headline states the specific outcome of consuming the magnet, not just its name.
- One field (email) unless there's a specific reason to ask for more — every additional field
  reduces conversion.
- Show the actual asset (a mockup, a screenshot of a page, a preview) — abstraction reduces
  perceived value.
- No navigation away from the page other than the opt-in form; remove every exit that isn't
  the conversion.

## After the opt-in

The delivery moment (thank-you page + first email) is part of the magnet, not an
afterthought — see `playbooks/email-marketing.md`'s welcome sequence for what happens next.
