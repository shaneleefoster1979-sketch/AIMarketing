# The Fundamentals: The Funnel

Every marketing task fits somewhere in this sequence. Identify the stage before producing any
output — the right copy for one stage is often the wrong copy for another.

```
AWARENESS  →  LEAD MAGNET  →  NURTURE  →  OFFER  →  CLOSE  →  RETAIN / REFER
```

## 1. Awareness

Goal: get a specific person's attention for the first time.

- Channels: organic content, paid ads, SEO, partnerships, word of mouth.
- The job here is a pattern interrupt + a promise, not a sale. Nobody buys from a cold
  audience on the first touch — asking them to is the most common funnel mistake.
- Success metric: reach and engagement among the *right* audience, not raw volume.

## 2. Lead Magnet (the opt-in)

Goal: convert an anonymous stranger into a known contact (email, phone, or retargeting
audience) by trading a specific, fast win for their attention.

- The magnet should solve one narrow problem completely, not summarize the whole topic.
- It should be consumable in minutes, not hours — a checklist beats a course at this stage.
- The magnet is also a qualifier: it should attract the buyer, not just anyone curious.
- See `playbooks/lead-magnets.md`.

## 3. Nurture

Goal: build enough trust and desire that the offer, when it arrives, feels like the obvious
next step rather than a cold pitch.

- Deliver value before asking for anything again.
- Tell stories that make the mechanism believable (case studies, before/afters, the "aha").
- Handle objections before they're raised — most churn happens from unspoken doubts, not
  stated ones.
- See `playbooks/email-marketing.md` and `playbooks/content-strategy.md`.

## 4. Offer

Goal: present a specific, priced solution to a specific problem, to a warmed-up audience.

- The offer statement should be a single sentence a customer could repeat back correctly.
- Bundle to increase perceived value; don't just discount to increase conversion.
- Every offer needs a reason to act now (a real deadline, real scarcity, or a real bonus
  window) — invented urgency erodes trust the moment it's noticed.
- See `playbooks/sales-letters.md` and `playbooks/paid-ads.md`.

## 5. Close

Goal: remove every remaining piece of friction between "I want this" and "I bought this."

- Minimize form fields, steps, and decisions at checkout.
- Restate the guarantee and the core promise right where the buy button is.
- Follow up on abandoned carts/checkouts within hours, not days.

## 6. Retain / Refer

Goal: turn a customer into a repeat customer and a referral source.

- Onboarding is a marketing asset: a customer who gets a fast first win is far less likely to
  churn or refund.
- Ask for the referral/testimonial at the moment of peak satisfaction (right after a win), not
  on a generic post-purchase timer.
- The cheapest new customer is a second purchase from an existing one — always check whether
  a retention fix beats an acquisition fix before spending more on ads.

## Using this model

When given any marketing task, name which stage(s) it touches before producing output. A
task that tries to do awareness-copy and close-copy in the same asset ("Follow us! Also buy
now!") usually needs to be split into two assets instead of written as one.
